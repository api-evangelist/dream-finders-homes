If you have been thinking about moving to Florida, you are not alone. People relocate to Florida for a lot of reasons, including career opportunities, warmer weather, and a lifestyle that makes it easier to spend time outdoors year-round. The key is choosing the right area for your day-to-day life and building a budget that matches the reality of where you want to live.This guide covers the big topics that matter most when you are relocating to FL: cost of living basics, taxes, climate, moving tips, and what to know about buying a new construction home. What does it really cost to live in Florida?Florida can be a great value compared to many high-cost states, but costs still vary a lot by region, commute, and lifestyle. When you plan your budget, it helps to break things into a few categories.HousingHousing is usually the largest line item. Florida has a wide mix, from low-maintenance townhomes close to city centers to larger single-family homes in master-planned communities. Your monthly payment can look very different in a coastal area versus a growing suburb, so it is smart to choose your target city first, then shop neighborhoods within it.Utilities and day-to-day expensesAir conditioning is a real part of Florida life, especially in summer, so utilities can be higher during warm months. Many buyers like the idea of new construction because modern building practices and energy-efficient features can help support comfort and potentially reduce energy use over time. Dream Finders Homes highlights energy-efficient features as a benefit of buying new. TaxesTaxes are a major reason many people move to Florida. Florida does not have a state income tax, and the state constitution forbids it, which is one reason Florida often appeals to people relocating from higher-tax states. You will still want to plan for other taxes, including:• Sales tax: Florida’s general state sales tax rate is 6%, and local surtaxes may apply depending on the county. • Property taxes: Property taxes vary by county, and Florida offers homeowner benefits like the homestead exemption and the Save Our Homes assessment limitation for eligible primary residences. A quick tip if you are relocating to FL: if you plan to make Florida your primary residence, learn the local rules and deadlines for filing for exemptions in the county where you buy.Best places to live in FloridaWhen people say they want to relocate Florida, what they usually mean is they want a specific version of Florida. Big city energy, beach-town pace, family-friendly suburbs, golf and outdoor living, or easy access to travel and entertainment. Pick your target metro first, then narrow to neighborhoods. Your commute, school preferences, and lifestyle goals will matter more than a statewide “best place” list.2. Budget for the “first month” expenses. Think utility deposits, moving supplies, temporary housing if needed, and basic setup costs.3. Plan your Florida timeline. If you are switching jobs, leasing temporarily, or buying new construction, map out realistic milestones for tours, financing, and move-in.4. Build a short list of must-haves. For example: a first-floor primary suite, home office, garage space, or low-maintenance living.5. Schedule trips with purpose. If you can, combine community tours with drive-time tests.

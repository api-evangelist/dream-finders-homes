When searching for your dream home, it’s easy to get swept up in the beauty of a modern kitchen or the perfect layout of a primary suite. However, Dream Finders Homes believes that a strong community is built on a solid foundation. And that includes understanding the financial structure behind your neighborhood. Choosing between a Homeowners Association vs. Community Development District structure is an important decision to make regarding the long-term value of your investment.Understanding new home community costs is about more than just the mortgage. It’s about knowing how your neighborhood’s infrastructure is maintained and how the HOA monthly fees explained at the closing table might change over time. Whether you’re looking for a bustling urban hub or a quiet suburban retreat, being educated on the HOA vs CDD models will help you choose the lifestyle that fits your budget and your future.What Is an HOA (Homeowners Association)?A Homeowners Association (HOA) is a private organization typically formed by a real estate developer to manage and maintain a community. When you buy a home in an HOA-governed area, you automatically become a member and are required to pay dues.In the cost of HOA communities, these fees are primarily used to manage the lifestyle and operations of the neighborhood. This includes:Maintenance: Keeping common areas like entrances and clubhouses clean.Landscaping: Mowing the grass in shared parks or along the main streets.Amenities: Funding the day-to-day operations of pools, fitness centers, or playgrounds.Pros of HOA CommunitiesThe primary benefit of an HOA-only model is its simple structure. You have one point of contact for neighborhood rules and maintenance. For smaller communities, this can provide a sense of immediate order and care for the shared spaces.Cons of HOA CommunitiesOne of the main drawbacks is that in an HOA-only model, everything must be covered. Because these associations don't have access to the same long-term financing as a government-backed district, they often rely on higher monthly dues to cover both daily chores and major repairs. Furthermore, because private boards manage them, these dues can sometimes increase unpredictably if the association hasn't saved enough for a rainy day.What Is a CDD (Community Development District)?If you’ve found yourself asking, "What is a CDD?" you aren't alone. A Community Development District (CDD) is a special-purpose government unit created to finance, build, and operate high-quality infrastructure. While an HOA is a private entity, a CDD is a public-private partnership that ensures a community’s essential infrastructure—like roads and major utilities—is built to a higher standard from day one.How CDD Fees WorkInstead of paying a massive monthly bill to a private association, HOA fees vs CDD fees are structured differently. CDD costs are typically split into two parts: a bond portion (to pay back the initial construction of infrastructure) and an operations/maintenance portion. These are conveniently included as a line item on your annual property tax bill.This creates a more predictable and transparent cost structure. Because the infrastructure is funded upfront through long-term bonds, homeowners don’t have to worry about the neighborhood lacking essential features like paved roads or working streetlights when they move in. Everything is fully functional the day you arrive.HOA vs CDD Comparison: Key Differences at a GlanceTo help simplify your HOA vs CDD comparison, we’ve broken down how these two models typically function side-by-side:FeatureTypical HOA-Only ModelCDD + HOA ModelPayment MethodMonthly or Quarterly check to the HOALine item on property tax bill (CDD) + small HOA feeInfrastructureOften lags; construction noise can last for yearsFully functioning roads and utilities from Day 1Cost PredictabilityDues can increase unpredictablyFees are clearly outlined and long-termPrimary FocusDaily maintenance and rulesMajor infrastructure and amenitiesWhy Some Builders Use a CDD ModelAt Dream Finders Homes, we utilize the CDD model in many of our new home communities in Jacksonville and beyond because of the CDD community benefits it provides to our homeowners.By using a CDD, we can ensure that major utilities and roads are funded upfront. This minimizes the risk of unexpected assessments (those dreaded surprise bills that HOA-only communities sometimes send out when a major pipe bursts or a road needs repaving). Perhaps the best part? Because the CDD covers the big stuff, we can often keep your monthly HOA fees much smaller.Which Is Better for Homebuyers?Choosing between an HOA vs CDD depends on your lifestyle priorities.Choose HOA-only if you prefer a very small, simple community and don’t mind the potential for fluctuating monthly costs.Choose a CDD model if you want a master-planned community with top-tier amenities, predictable tax-based payments, and the assurance that your neighborhood’s infrastructure is handled by a professional government entity rather than a group of neighbors.FAQs: Your New Home Community Costs AnsweredWhat is the difference between HOA and CDD?An HOA is a private entity that manages community rules and basic maintenance. A CDD is a public-purpose district that funds and maintains major infrastructure like roads and utilities through property taxes.Is a CDD fee better than an HOA fee?Many buyers prefer CDD fees because they are predictable and allow for lower HOA dues. It also ensures the community amenities are high-quality from the moment you move in.Do you pay both HOA and CDD?In many cases, yes. The CDD handles the large-scale infrastructure, while a small HOA fee covers things like enforcing community standards and smaller aesthetic details.Are CDD fees permanent?The bond portion of a CDD fee is eventually paid off (typically over 20-30 years). Once the bond is retired, that portion of your tax bill disappears, leaving only the smaller operations and maintenance fee.Why do some communities have lower HOA fees?Communities that utilize a CDD often have significantly lower HOA fees because the heavy lifting of road and utility maintenance is handled by the district, not the association.Final Thoughts: Making the Right ChoiceAt Dream Finders Homes, we are your partner in the homebuying journey.
